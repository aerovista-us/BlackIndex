# BI-PAT-003 — Fragmented oversight / incomplete-chain review

## Definition
Compartmentation, distributed ownership, or narrow review prevents any independent reviewer from seeing the complete collection/event → interpretation → approval → action → outcome chain.

## Evidence base
- Operation Northwoods: consequential deception proposals were developed in restricted military planning channels; the reviewed record does not itself establish later civilian approval or execution.
- COINTELPRO Alexandria: field-office proposals, headquarters routing, third-party influence, and results reporting create multi-party action chains that a target or isolated reviewer may not see end-to-end.
- Church Committee Book II: the Senate review documents deficiencies in control, accountability, oversight, dissemination, retention, and cumulative program review.

## Indicators
- No single authorized reviewer can reconstruct the full decision chain.
- Operational units review their own evidence and outcomes without independent challenge.
- Apparently independent incidents/actions share hidden upstream ownership.
- Proposal disposition is missing or inaccessible.

## Controls
- Full-chain auditability for authorized oversight.
- Independent high-impact review outside the operational chain.
- Explicit proposal/approval/execution/outcome states.
- Cross-program correlation review that preserves necessary compartmentation while exposing common control points.

## Confidence
**High** across three reviewed sources.

## Guardrail
Compartmentation is not inherently improper; the risk arises when it prevents meaningful accountability or independent review of consequential action.
