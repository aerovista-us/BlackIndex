# MINARET — NSA Watch List Activity

- **Evidence status:** reviewed

## CLAIM
NSA used U.S.-person names systematically as selectors in watch-list activity from 1967–1973, including communications involving Americans where one communicator was abroad.

## DOCUMENT SAYS
Lew Allen's testimony describes a 1967–1973 NSA activity responding to requirements from other agencies. It states that U.S. names were used systematically to select messages and that the activity included some communications between U.S. citizens when one communicator was at a foreign location. NSA historical material later identifies this watch-list activity as MINARET and records that the project expanded to domestic dissidents and prominent public figures.

The testimony also shows that the program operated inside the foreign-intelligence collection system while selectors and dissemination requirements created the domestic-rights problem.

## DOES NOT PROVE
- It does not establish that NSA indiscriminately recorded all domestic communications.
- It does not mean every watch-listed person was targeted for the same reason or under the same authority.
- It does not by itself establish criminal liability for specific officials.

## ANOMALIES
- The activity depended heavily on requirements supplied by outside agencies, diffusing responsibility.
- Portions of the historical testimony/archive remained classified or compartmented.
- Later NSA history states reports were distributed using unusual procedures that obscured NSA as the originating agency.

## Redaction / archive analysis
Classified sections affect precise requirement chains and operational details, but the existence and basic mechanism of the watch-list activity are well established. Redactions should be scored around selector origin, dissemination, and authorization rather than treated generically.

## Mechanisms / patterns
- `BI-PAT-001` Purpose drift — foreign-intelligence infrastructure repurposed around U.S.-person selectors.
- `BI-PAT-002` Collection-to-consequence chain — selector input from one agency drives collection/reporting by another.
- `BI-PAT-003` Fragmented oversight — requirements, collection, and use distributed across organizations.
- **New candidate:** `PAT-CAND-009` Selector-layer policy bypass — otherwise legitimate bulk/foreign collection becomes rights-sensitive through selector choice and downstream dissemination.

## Candidate controls
- U.S.-person selector approval and legal basis must be explicit and independently reviewable.
- Requirement originator, selector rationale, collection authority, dissemination recipients, and expiration must be linked in one audit chain.
- Watch lists require expiry and periodic revalidation.

## Candidate detections
- U.S.-person selector enters a foreign-intelligence system without a separately recorded authority.
- Same selector is requested by multiple agencies but no single owner holds full accountability.
- Reports suppress or obscure the originating collection agency without a documented lawful need.

## Review disposition
**Reviewed.** The core surveillance mechanism is confirmed; narrower claims about individual targets require target-specific records.
