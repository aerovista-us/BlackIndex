## CLAIM
Private communications companies supplied substantial amounts of telegraphed information to the U.S. government over many years, and NSA's downstream use of some of that material became an oversight and civil-liberties issue.

## DOCUMENT SAYS
NSA's own summary states that private firms supplied substantial amounts of telegraphed information to the government beginning in World War II and continuing through the Korean, Cold War, and Southeast Asia periods. NSA states that the government requested the information and that firms provided it with assurances of national-security purpose.

The accompanying Lew Allen / Bella Abzug correspondence shows active congressional inquiry into NSA/FBI monitoring and interception of cable and telex traffic and NSA's attempt to keep sensitive communications-intelligence details out of open hearings.

## DOES NOT PROVE
- It does not establish that private companies understood every downstream NSA use.
- It does not prove that all traffic supplied was read, retained, or disseminated.
- It does not by itself resolve the legality of every phase of SHAMROCK.

## ANOMALIES
- Corporate provision and intelligence use were separated, creating an accountability gap between source access and downstream selection.
- The record shows tension between public oversight and secrecy claims.
- Precise scope must be reconstructed from additional Church Committee and NSA records.

Primary source: NSA SHAMROCK summary and Allen/Abzug correspondence, October 1975, pp. 1–3.

## Redaction / archive analysis
The short packet is not primarily a redaction case. Its value is chain-of-custody and role separation: provider, collector, selector, analyst, and consumer may be different actors.

- **Candidate:** `PAT-CAND-010` Third-party access normalization — recurring voluntary/informal access can become institutionalized without the source party seeing downstream uses.

## Candidate controls
- Data provided by third parties must carry explicit permitted-use and retention constraints.
- Downstream intelligence use requires auditable purpose, selector basis, and recipient chain.
- Long-running informal access arrangements require periodic legal and policy reauthorization.

## Candidate detections
- Data source remains constant while downstream uses expand materially.
- High-volume third-party feed lacks per-use accountability or retention policy.
- Oversight records show repeated inability to identify what subset of supplied data was actually used.
