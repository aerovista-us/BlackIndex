## CLAIM

This FBI Electronic Communication is relevant to the question of whether persons encountered by Nawaf al-Hazmi and Khalid al-Mihdhar in Southern California knowingly or unknowingly provided logistical assistance, what relationships connected those persons to one another, and how the FBI characterized that evidence by April 2016.

The EC is also relevant as a bridge between underlying interviews/analytical products and later Operation Encore summaries. It should be treated as an FBI synthesis layer, not as independent corroboration of every underlying fact it summarizes.

## DOCUMENT CONTENT

### Investigation status and 2015 interviews

The EC states that a full investigation had been initiated on **October 26, 2007**. It describes 2015 interviews conducted while reviewing a subject's citizenship applications and prior statements concerning contacts with persons who provided logistical support to Hazmi and Mihdhar. The EC identifies **Omar al-Bayoumi** and **Fahad al-Thumairy** among persons examined in that context. See PDF pp. 3–4.

### Thumairy / King Fahad Mosque context

The EC contains a section concerning communications and relationships involving persons connected to Hazmi and Mihdhar and describes one individual as a close associate of **Fahad al-Thumairy**, identified as imam of the King Fahad Mosque. The record also discusses indirect telephone connections and questions posed in later interviews about possible prior knowledge or facilitation. Much of the surrounding identity and communications detail is redacted. See PDF pp. 7–8.

### Bayoumi encounter and assistance

The EC recounts the FBI's reconstruction of **Omar al-Bayoumi's** encounter with Hazmi and Mihdhar after a meeting at the Saudi consulate / Los Angeles-area context and the subsequent move to a restaurant. It summarizes earlier statements and later investigative questioning concerning whether that meeting was accidental or preceded by other contact. See PDF pp. 8–10.

The EC further summarizes forms of assistance attributed in the investigative record to Bayoumi after meeting Hazmi and Mihdhar, including housing/logistical help and other practical support. These descriptions are presented as FBI investigative synthesis and should be linked back to the underlying interviews, financial records, leases, telephone records, and source documents before being counted as independently established facts. See PDF pp. 9–10.

### Communications analysis

Several pages summarize FBI communications analysis and record checks involving Bayoumi, Hazmi, Mihdhar, Bin Attash and other persons or identifiers. The visible text indicates reliance on historical telephone records, subscriber information, and other analytical products. Significant portions are redacted as FBI classified, PII, grand-jury-derived, or withheld at another U.S. Government agency's direction. See PDF pp. 10–13.

### Later travel / contact reporting

The latter pages contain reporting about contacts, travel and telephone activity involving Bayoumi and other persons, including calls during the period when the hijackers were in Southern California. The EC also summarizes earlier FBI interviews and source reporting concerning Khalid al-Mihdhar, Hazmi, and other al-Qaida-linked persons. Several pivotal details are obscured by USG and PII redactions. See PDF pp. 15–17.
