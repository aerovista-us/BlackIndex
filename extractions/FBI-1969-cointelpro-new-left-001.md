## Evidence established by the packet

1. The packet is an FBI Freedom of Information / Privacy Acts reproduction labeled `COINTELPRO — NEW LEFT — ALEXANDRIA — 100-449698-101`.
2. Memoranda from the Alexandria field office to FBI headquarters use recurring sections for **Potential Counterintelligence Action**, **Pending Counterintelligence Action**, and **Tangible Results**, showing that disruption proposals and outcomes were formally tracked.
3. A 30 March 1971 memorandum anticipates upcoming Washington demonstrations and states Alexandria would remain alert for potential counterintelligence situations; it also reports no pending action and no tangible results at that time.
4. A 30 September 1970 memorandum discusses developing background information on the owner of a building used by a commune and, if useful, recommending an interview that could lead the owner to consider eviction.
5. A 26 March 1970 memorandum describes investigation of a New Left group and an effort to uncover possible financial irregularities, while again reporting no pending action and no tangible results.
6. September 1969 material discusses using local press exposure and possible additional legal pressure against an identified activist. The packet includes language describing the program purpose as neutralizing New Left organizations and leaders and recommends exploiting an identified incident for exposure.
7. The packet contains redactions and degraded reproductions; the FBI cover notice states that blurred/light/difficult pages reflect the condition or color of the originals and are the best copies available.

## What the packet does **not** establish

- It does not establish that every proposed counterintelligence action was approved or executed.
- Several memoranda expressly state that no action was pending or that no tangible results had been achieved at the reporting date.
- Redactions prevent full identification of some persons, sources, and operational details.
- The packet is a small Alexandria slice of the larger COINTELPRO-New Left program and should not be generalized to every office or activity without corroboration.

## Corroboration

- Internal file numbers, dates, routing, and repeated references to Bureau-wide COINTELPRO-New Left instructions tie the packet to an organized FBI program rather than isolated field-office correspondence.
- The packet repeatedly references the Bureau letter to all offices dated 10 May 1968, indicating a common program framework.

## Inferences

### High confidence

- Intelligence collection was used not only to observe targets but to identify potential leverage for disruption, exposure, reputational harm, or third-party pressure.
- Field offices were expected to report both proposed actions and measurable outcomes, creating an institutional feedback loop around disruption activity.

### Moderate confidence

- The repeated requirement to identify potential actions may create pressure to transform collected information into intervention even when investigative value is weak.

### Unsupported / do not infer

- Do not assume every redaction hides misconduct; redactions may protect identities, sources, privacy, or other protected information.
- Do not infer that every proposed action in this packet occurred unless the record documents execution or tangible results.

## Mechanisms / patterns

### BI-PATTERN-CANDIDATE: Collection-to-leverage conversion

**Mechanism:** Information gathered through intelligence/investigative processes is repurposed into leverage for disruption, exposure, eviction, employment consequences, legal pressure, or other non-neutral outcomes.

**Signal:** A record moves from describing a subject to asking how that information can be used against the subject or associated organization.

**Confidence:** High.

### BI-PATTERN-CANDIDATE: Third-party pressure

**Mechanism:** Authorities seek outcomes indirectly by influencing landlords, employers, media, family members, institutions, or other intermediaries rather than acting directly.

**Signal:** Recommendations to approach a property owner, feed information to media, or induce another institution/person to take action.

**Confidence:** High.

### BI-PATTERN-CANDIDATE: Disruption performance loop

**Mechanism:** An organization tracks proposed action, pending action, and tangible results, turning disruption into a managed performance category.

**Signal:** Standardized recurring reporting fields for potential actions and measurable results.

**Confidence:** High.

## Failure modes

- **Purpose drift:** intelligence gathered for one purpose is reused for punitive or disruptive ends.
- **Weak predicate for intervention:** lawful or political association becomes a gateway to searching for exploitable vulnerabilities.
- **Indirect accountability gap:** harmful outcomes can be produced through third parties, making responsibility harder to see.
- **Metric pressure:** requiring recurring “results” can incentivize action where restraint would otherwise be appropriate.
- **Information asymmetry:** targeted individuals may never know that government-supplied information influenced landlords, media, employers, or other institutions.

## Operational analogs

These are governance analogies, not assertions of equivalence:

- Security telemetry repurposed to discipline employees without a documented policy basis.
- Customer or member data used to pressure external partners against an individual outside the original collection purpose.
- Fraud/risk teams rewarded for adverse actions rather than accurate classification, creating pressure toward escalation.
- Internal intelligence functions quietly seeding information to third parties to obtain outcomes they could not directly authorize.

## Candidate controls

1. **Purpose-binding:** require explicit documented authority before information collected for intelligence/security purposes can be used for materially different adverse action.
2. **Secondary-use approval:** high-impact secondary use should require independent legal/policy review and an auditable justification.
3. **Third-party influence register:** log when internal information is intentionally provided to an external party to induce action against a person or organization.
4. **Outcome-neutral performance metrics:** measure analytical accuracy and risk reduction rather than number of adverse/disruptive actions.
5. **Action/proposal distinction:** systems must clearly distinguish suggested, approved, attempted, completed, and effective actions.

## Candidate detections

- Intelligence or case records followed by outbound contact to landlords, employers, press, vendors, or family without a documented approved purpose.
- Repeated adverse-action proposals generated from association/political/activity metadata rather than conduct-based predicates.
- Teams with unusually high rates of “potential action” submissions but weak evidence of underlying policy violations.
- Case systems where “success” or “tangible results” correlate primarily with adverse outcomes rather than validated risk reduction.

## Training scenario

### Scenario: Useful information, wrong purpose

An internal security analyst discovers embarrassing but non-security-related information while investigating a legitimate alert. A manager suggests quietly passing it to an outside partner who could terminate the subject’s access or relationship.

Trainees must identify the purpose shift, stop the secondary use, preserve the original investigative record, require independent authorization, and document the attempted escalation.

**Teaching point:** possession of information does not create authority to use it for every available purpose.
