# Justification for US Military Intervention in Cuba — Operation Northwoods

- **Doc ID:** `JCS-1962-operation-northwoods-001`
- **Call ID:** `CALL-006`
- **Native ID:** `JCS 1969/321`
- **Source:** Joint Chiefs of Staff / National Security Archive copy
- **Document date:** 1962-03-13
- **Evidence status:** reviewed
- **Canonical landing page:** https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/CMC-60/joint-chiefs-pretexts-to-invade-Cuba-1962
- **Artifact:** https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/sites/default/files/2022-10/Joint-Chiefs-pretexts-to-invade-Cuba-March-1962_0.pdf

## Evidence established by the document

1. The Joint Chiefs of Staff considered and forwarded a set of proposed pretexts intended to provide justification for U.S. military intervention in Cuba. The cover memorandum characterizes the submission as preliminary and suitable for planning purposes.
2. The attached planning paper explicitly describes a desired integrated, time-phased plan in which incidents could be combined with seemingly unrelated events to create the appearance of Cuban rashness and aggression and place the United States in the apparent position of suffering defensible grievances.
3. The annex proposes coordinated deceptive or staged incidents around Guantanamo, including staged attacks, sabotage, simulated hostile activity, and fabricated or manipulated attribution.
4. Other proposals include a staged or simulated ship incident, a manufactured terror campaign attributed to Cuba, simulated Cuban operations against neighboring states, use of aircraft made to appear Cuban, and a simulated civil-aircraft shootdown scenario.
5. The paper recommends that responsibility for military and paramilitary aspects of the proposed plan, including overt and covert military operations, be assigned to the Joint Chiefs of Staff.

## What the document does **not** establish

- It does **not** establish that the listed proposals were approved for execution.
- It does **not** establish that the proposed incidents were actually carried out.
- It does **not** establish that every idea originated with or was endorsed by every senior U.S. official outside the documented JCS process.
- The document itself repeatedly describes the material as a preliminary planning submission and individual projects as candidates for case-by-case consideration.

## Corroboration

- The National Security Archive identifies the memorandum as a Joint Chiefs of Staff document dated 13 March 1962 and notes its later declassification through records opened by the JFK Assassination Records Review Board.
- The document internally references Operation MONGOOSE and prior requests for possible pretexts connected with the Cuba Project.

## Inferences

### High confidence

- Senior military planning channels were willing to formally document deceptive pretext concepts as possible means of generating public and political justification for intervention.
- The proposed mechanism depended not only on an incident itself, but on attribution, information release, public perception, and cumulative narrative effects.

### Moderate confidence

- The planning model illustrates how an institution can treat perception management and operational action as parts of the same decision architecture when seeking authorization for a desired end state.

### Unsupported / do not infer

- Do not infer that a later event sharing superficial characteristics with a Northwoods proposal was therefore caused by the same actors or plan. Similarity is not provenance.

## Mechanisms / patterns

### BI-PATTERN-CANDIDATE: Desired-end-state pretext design

**Mechanism:** Define a desired policy outcome first, then construct incidents or evidence pathways capable of supplying the justification required to reach it.

**Signal:** Planning language starts with the justification needed for a predetermined action rather than with neutral evaluation of observed events.

**Confidence:** High for this document.

### BI-PATTERN-CANDIDATE: Cumulative incident narrative

**Mechanism:** Multiple individually ambiguous or staged events are coordinated so their cumulative effect creates a stronger public/political narrative than any single event could.

**Signal:** Time-phased incident planning, correlated actions, synchronized information release, or emphasis on cumulative public perception.

**Confidence:** High.

### BI-PATTERN-CANDIDATE: Attribution as an operational component

**Mechanism:** Operational planning includes deliberate control over who appears responsible for an event.

**Signal:** Prepared documents, disguised equipment/personnel, staged hostile actors, controlled media exposure, or other mechanisms designed to shape attribution.

**Confidence:** High.

## Failure modes

- **Outcome-first analysis:** desired policy action biases the design and interpretation of supporting evidence.
- **Collapsed operational/information boundaries:** the same plan controls event generation, attribution, and public interpretation.
- **Insufficient independent challenge:** highly consequential deceptive proposals can progress within restricted planning channels without broad adversarial review.
- **Compartmentation risk:** narrow distribution can protect legitimate secrecy but also reduce the number of independent reviewers able to challenge assumptions or legality.

## Operational analogs

These are governance analogies, not assertions of equivalence:

- Security teams fabricating or selectively presenting evidence to justify a predetermined enforcement action.
- Incident-response processes where the desired attribution is chosen before the evidence review is complete.
- Executives creating artificial urgency or staged demonstrations to secure approval for an already-selected project.
- Automated systems whose evidence-generation and decision/approval functions are controlled by the same actor without independent verification.

## Candidate controls

1. **Separate evidence generation from decision authorization.** The party producing or collecting evidence should not have unilateral authority to determine the consequential action justified by it.
2. **Require adversarial review for high-impact attribution.** A materially independent reviewer should test alternative explanations before escalation.
3. **Record decision directionality.** Audit whether the process began with observed evidence and derived an action, or began with a desired action and searched for justification.
4. **Require provenance for attribution evidence.** Claims used to justify consequential action must remain traceable to original evidence and transformations.
5. **Escalate deception proposals.** Any authorized simulation/red-team/deception activity must be clearly bounded, logged, and prevented from contaminating operational evidence used for real-world decisions.

## Candidate detections

- Decision documents that contain outcome language before supporting evidence is established.
- Repeated reuse of the same team/account/system for evidence creation, attribution, approval, and public reporting.
- Evidence artifacts created shortly before a decision and lacking independent provenance.
- Simulated/test events crossing into production evidence stores without unmistakable labeling.
- High-impact incident clusters whose attribution relies primarily on centrally prepared rather than independently collected evidence.

## Training scenario

### Scenario: The incident that fits too well

A team receives several apparently unrelated alerts that collectively support a major enforcement action leadership already favors. Trainees must determine whether the alerts are independent observations or products of a shared source, preserve provenance, construct alternative hypotheses, and require independent approval before acting.

**Teaching point:** Cumulative evidence is only stronger when the components are genuinely independent and their provenance is understood.

## Watch-outs / alternative explanations

- Military organizations routinely generate contingency plans, including options never approved or executed. The existence of planning material is evidence of consideration, not execution.
- Highly classified distribution alone is not evidence of improper intent; sensitive military planning is normally compartmented. The content and decision process must be evaluated separately from the classification markings.
- Historical context matters: the paper was produced during intense U.S.-Cuba confrontation and Operation MONGOOSE planning. Context explains the planning environment but does not erase the significance of the documented proposals.
