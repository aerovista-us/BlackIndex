## CLAIM

GAO reports that the E-130J low-rate production decision moved by approximately one year as system-integration risks materialized. It identifies mission-system weight reduction for C-130J-30 integration as one example.

GAO also records disagreement with the program office over iterative-development and digital-engineering alignment. The program office asserted alignment; GAO states supporting documentation was not provided and that its analysis of program documentation pointed the other way.

These are attributed GAO findings and program-office responses.

## DOCUMENT CONTENT

The E-130J assessment is on report pages 139–140. GAO describes the December 2024 EMD award and states that a September 2024 independent technical risk assessment had highlighted integration complexity.

GAO reports contractors were modifying existing mission systems to reduce weight. It also says the program was not pursuing a minimum viable product and had reduced some digital-thread efforts.

The development-practices and software state is explicitly current as of January 2026. This source therefore predates the April 2026 PB2027 MSAR state.

## SOURCE ATTRIBUTION

GAO is an independent legislative-branch oversight agency. Its analysis is institutionally independent, while many underlying E-130J facts derive from program documents, questionnaires, or program officials.

## CORROBORATION

The later PB2027 E-130J MSAR records IBR closure and AV1 configuration PDR completion, retains full-system PDR as future, and reports zero aircraft delivered. The April 2026 Navy FY2027 BA5 separately records a revised current schedule.

These sources address different dates and propositions.

## CONFLICTS

GAO records a disagreement with the program office over acquisition approach and supporting documentation. The later MSAR statement that the program was on track does not automatically resolve GAO's earlier risk analysis.
