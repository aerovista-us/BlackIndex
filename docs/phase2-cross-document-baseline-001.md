# Phase 2 Cross-Document Baseline 001

Reviewed sources:

1. `SENATE-1976-church-committee-001` — Church Committee Book II
2. `JCS-1962-operation-northwoods-001` — Operation Northwoods / JCS Cuba pretext planning
3. `FBI-1969-cointelpro-new-left-001` — COINTELPRO New Left, Alexandria

## Purpose

This baseline tests whether candidate BlackIndex patterns survive comparison across independent source types. A pattern is not promoted merely because it is dramatic or because one document states it clearly. Cross-source convergence, mechanism similarity, and important differences are recorded separately.

## Strongest convergence

### 1. Purpose drift / information-to-action amplification

**Church Committee:** identifies risk not only in collection but in dissemination, retention, and covert action; finds that domestic intelligence targets expanded into lawful activity and that information could be used beyond the original investigative purpose.

**COINTELPRO Alexandria:** provides concrete field-office examples of information being evaluated for operational leverage—media exposure, possible landlord pressure, financial-vulnerability investigation—and recurring reporting on potential action and tangible results.

**Assessment:** Strong cross-source corroboration for a reusable governance pattern.

**Candidate reusable pattern:** Information collected under one authority or purpose can migrate into a broader adverse-action workflow unless secondary use is explicitly constrained.

**Promotion status:** `cross-source-supported / implementation-review-required`

---

### 2. Weak separation between evidence/intelligence and consequential action

**Church Committee:** recommends defined predicates, controls on intrusive techniques, dissemination limits, Attorney General supervision, and stronger authorization/termination controls.

**COINTELPRO Alexandria:** shows the same program environment evaluating collected information for disruption and third-party influence.

**Northwoods:** takes the separation problem in a different direction: the planning record begins with a desired policy objective—adequate justification for intervention—and considers how incidents, attribution, and public perception could be constructed to support it.

**Assessment:** The sources do not describe the same program or legal problem, but all three demonstrate the value of separating evidence generation, analytical judgment, and authorization of consequential action.

**Candidate control:** Independent review between evidence-producing functions and high-impact decision authorization.

**Promotion status:** `candidate-control / cross-source-supported`

## Distinct pattern: outcome-first justification

**Primary source:** Northwoods.

The Northwoods planning paper explicitly seeks pretexts that could supply justification for an already-defined potential military intervention. It discusses cumulative incidents, controlled attribution, and international/public perception.

Church Committee and the Alexandria packet do not independently establish this exact mechanism, so BlackIndex should not falsely label it as three-source corroborated.

**Candidate pattern:** Desired-end-state pretext design.

**Promotion status:** `single-source-high-confidence / seek-corroboration`

**Next corroboration targets:** related Operation MONGOOSE records, the 5 March 1962 Cuba Project request, the 8 March 1962 naval memorandum on provoking military action, and disposition/approval records.

---

## Distinct pattern: managed disruption metrics

**Primary source:** COINTELPRO Alexandria.

Recurring sections for Potential Counterintelligence Action, Pending Counterintelligence Action, and Tangible Results show disruption being treated as a reportable operational category. Church Committee broadly corroborates covert disruption as a category of domestic intelligence abuse but does not, in this reviewed extraction, independently establish the same field-level metric structure.

**Candidate pattern:** Disruption performance loop.

**Promotion status:** `partially-corroborated / seek additional FBI field-office records`

## Distinct pattern: oversight fragmentation and termination failure

**Primary source:** Church Committee Book II.

The Committee identifies deficiencies across executive, Attorney General, congressional, and agency oversight and emphasizes stronger authorization and termination controls.

The other two sources provide examples of sensitive programs/planning but do not by themselves establish the full oversight-fragmentation mechanism.

**Candidate pattern:** Oversight fragmentation.

**Promotion status:** `single-review-source / broad historical corroboration expected`

## Redaction and missing-record discipline

BlackIndex should treat missing context as an explicit analytical variable rather than an invitation to speculate.

- **COINTELPRO Alexandria:** person-level names and some operational passages are redacted; degraded originals create transcription uncertainty.
- **Northwoods:** the reviewed record itself references related memoranda and submissions not contained in the 15-page artifact. Missing approval/disposition records are material to any claim about what happened after proposal.
- **Church Committee:** the report summarizes a much larger evidentiary record; episode-specific claims should be traced into hearings, exhibits, staff reports, and underlying agency records.

### Redaction/context scoring rule

For each significant gap record:

1. **Location** — where the omission/redaction occurs.
2. **Likely information type** — identity, method, source, approval, operational detail, etc., only where context supports that characterization.
3. **Interpretive leverage** — low / medium / high: could the withheld material substantially change how the surrounding passage is understood?
4. **Incrimination potential** — unknown unless context provides a defensible basis; never assume a redaction is incriminating merely because it exists.
5. **Recovery path** — related file number, archive series, later release, hearing, court record, or corroborating collection.

## What we should **not** claim

- Northwoods proves that the proposed incidents were executed.
- A redaction is evidence of wrongdoing.
- Similarity between a later event and a historical proposal establishes shared authorship or causation.
- Every COINTELPRO proposal in the Alexandria packet was approved or carried out.
- Findings in Church Committee Book II automatically establish the details of every underlying episode without checking the underlying record.

## Next collection target

### CIA Family Jewels

Current verified CIA artifact:

`https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/family%20jewels%5B15132295%5D.pdf`

The CIA's own historical material describes the Family Jewels as a roughly 700-page collection compiled in 1973 after DCI James Schlesinger asked employees to report activities they believed might be inconsistent with the Agency's charter. The artifact should be ingested as the next major stress test for BlackIndex normalization and review.

### Review questions for Family Jewels

- Which entries independently corroborate predicate/purpose drift?
- Which entries show internal recognition that activities may conflict with charter or law?
- What evidence exists of approval chains, senior awareness, termination, or corrective action?
- Where do redactions materially limit conclusions?
- Which items are allegations/recollections versus contemporaneous operational records?
- What mechanisms overlap with Church Committee findings, and which remain distinct?

## Baseline disposition

Three reviewed sources are sufficient to begin cross-source validation, but not enough to freeze a canonical pattern library. Continue ingestion and review through Family Jewels and VENONA before promoting the first five reusable patterns into stable BlackIndex pattern/control libraries.
