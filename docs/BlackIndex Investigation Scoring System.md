# BlackIndex Investigation Scoring System

## Purpose

BlackIndex evaluates controversial historical claims without treating either government records or conspiracy claims as automatically trustworthy.

Every investigation uses **three independent scores**:

1. **Plausibility** — Could the actor reasonably have done it?
2. **Evidence** — What evidence indicates they actually did it?
3. **Obstruction / Anomaly** — How compromised, contradictory, evasive, or unusual is the surviving record?

A fourth diagnostic, **Archive Confidence**, measures how complete the available record appears to be.

---

# 1. Plausibility Score

**Maximum: 15**

| Factor | Score | Question |
|---|---:|---|
| Motive | 0–5 | Was there a meaningful reason or benefit? |
| Capability | 0–5 | Did the actor have the resources, authority, access, or technical ability? |
| Opportunity | 0–5 | Could the actor realistically have acted at the relevant time/place? |

### Interpretation

- **0–4:** Low
- **5–8:** Possible
- **9–11:** Plausible
- **12–13:** Strong
- **14–15:** Exceptional

**Important:** Motive + capability + opportunity do not prove involvement.

---

# 2. Evidence Score

**Maximum: 30**

Score each category from **0–5**.

| Factor | Measures |
|---|---|
| Direct Documentary Evidence | Orders, communications, logs, reports, recordings |
| Independent Corroboration | Separate sources reaching the same fact |
| Physical / Technical Evidence | Forensics, technical records, physical evidence |
| Witness Evidence | Credible firsthand testimony |
| Financial / Logistical Evidence | Money, travel, personnel, equipment, communications |
| Timeline / Causal Fit | Whether established events support the hypothesis |

### Counter-Evidence

Strong evidence contradicting the hypothesis should reduce the Evidence Score.

The final score remains **0–30**.

### Interpretation

- **0–4:** Unsupported
- **5–11:** Weak
- **12–19:** Meaningful but unresolved
- **20–25:** Strongly supported
- **26–30:** Confirmed / exceptionally well supported

---

# 3. Obstruction / Anomaly Score

**Maximum: 20**

| Factor | Score | Examples |
|---|---:|---|
| Record Irregularities | 0–5 | Missing files, destroyed evidence, missing attachments, unexplained gaps |
| Contradictory Accounts | 0–5 | Changed testimony, conflicting agency accounts, internal/public discrepancies |
| Evasion / Non-Cooperation | 0–5 | Avoided questions, delayed compliance, withheld records, incomplete responses |
| Misleading / Concealment Behavior | 0–5 | False statements, selective disclosure, unusual secrecy, mischaracterization |

### Interpretation

- **0–3:** Normal
- **4–7:** Notable
- **8–12:** Significant
- **13–16:** Severe
- **17–20:** Exceptional

### Critical Rule

**Evidence of concealment is evidence of concealment.**

It is not automatically proof of the underlying allegation.

Destroyed records may establish that someone wanted something hidden without establishing exactly what was being hidden.

---

# Archive Confidence

**0–5**

Measures how complete and trustworthy the surviving archive appears.

| Score | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 5 | Extensive, intact, independently corroborated archive |
| 4 | Good record with minor gaps |
| 3 | Meaningful gaps |
| 2 | Major missing/classified/destroyed material |
| 1 | Severely compromised record |
| 0 | Archive effectively unavailable |

A lack of documentary evidence carries substantially less weight when Archive Confidence is low.

---

# Assessment Classes

### A — Confirmed
Direct evidence and substantial corroboration.

### B — Strongly Supported
Multiple independent lines of evidence with limited unresolved questions.

### C — Plausible / Unresolved
Meaningful evidence exists, but important gaps prevent a conclusion.

### D — Weak
Possible, but evidence depends heavily on inference or assumptions.

### E — Unsupported
Little meaningful evidence or substantial contradictory evidence.

### X — Indeterminate
The surviving record is too incomplete, destroyed, classified, or contradictory for a responsible conclusion.

**Plausibility or obstruction alone cannot upgrade an investigation to A or B.**

**Assessment:** `A / B / C / D / E / X`

### Strongest Evidence
The best evidence supporting the hypothesis.

### Strongest Counter-Evidence
The strongest evidence against it.

### Critical Missing Evidence
What would materially change the assessment?

### Anomaly Log
Record:

- Missing or destroyed documents
- Missing attachments
- Timeline inconsistencies
- Changed testimony
- Agency disagreements
- Unusual classification
- Delayed disclosure
- Refusals or evasive answers
- Lost evidence
- Public statements conflicting with internal records
- Procedural deviations
- Evidence appearing after earlier denials

---

# Core BlackIndex Rule

Separate:

**Documented Fact → Corroborated Inference → Unresolved Gap → Speculation**

Never convert:

**Could have + benefited + behaved strangely**

into:

**Therefore did it.**

Likewise, never convert:

**No surviving document says it happened**

into:

**Therefore it did not happen.**

BlackIndex should show what is known, what is strongly indicated, what remains missing, and exactly where inference begins.
