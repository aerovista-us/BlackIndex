# Phase 2 Cross-Document Synthesis 001

## Sources compared

1. `SENATE-1976-church-committee-001` — Church Committee Book II
2. `JCS-1962-operation-northwoods-001` — Operation Northwoods planning memorandum
3. `FBI-1969-cointelpro-new-left-001` — COINTELPRO New Left, Alexandria packet

## Method

This review compares mechanisms, not sensational surface similarities. A pattern is promoted only when at least two records independently support a materially similar governance failure or operational mechanism. Proposal, approval, execution, and outcome states remain distinct.

## Cross-document findings

### 1. Purpose drift / objective-first decisioning

**Northwoods:** planning begins with the desired endpoint — justification for military intervention — and develops incidents/evidence pathways capable of producing that justification.

**COINTELPRO:** information collected through intelligence/investigative activity is evaluated for use in disruption, exposure, eviction, legal pressure, or other adverse outcomes.

**Church Committee:** the Senate review describes domestic intelligence programs expanding beyond narrow predicates and emphasizes the cumulative risk to privacy, dissent, and constitutional rights.

**Common mechanism:** once an institution defines a target outcome, collection and analysis can drift from neutral fact-finding toward producing the predicate needed for action.

**Promotion:** Yes — reusable pattern.

### 2. Collection / event generation → attribution or leverage → consequential action

**Northwoods:** staged or deceptive incidents are paired with attribution design and public/international narrative effects intended to support intervention.

**COINTELPRO:** collected information is converted into third-party pressure or exposure intended to neutralize/disrupt targets.

**Church Committee:** the oversight record describes risks arising not only from collection but from dissemination, retention, covert action, and the cumulative interaction of programs.

**Common mechanism:** harm often arises downstream of collection/event generation — at the point information is attributed, disseminated, repurposed, or converted into action.

**Promotion:** Yes — reusable pattern.

### 3. Fragmented or narrow review increases risk

**Northwoods:** highly consequential deception concepts were developed within restricted military planning channels; the document itself does not establish broader civilian approval or execution.

**COINTELPRO:** field offices identify potential actions and report outcomes within an organized program; individual targets often lack visibility into the chain producing third-party consequences.

**Church Committee:** the Senate review repeatedly identifies weaknesses in control, accountability, oversight, dissemination, and retention.

**Common mechanism:** compartmentation or fragmented responsibility can be legitimate operationally, but becomes dangerous when no reviewer can see the full collection → decision → action chain.

**Promotion:** Yes — reusable pattern.

## Important non-patterns

### Superficial event similarity is not provenance

Northwoods documents proposed staged incidents. That does **not** make later incidents with superficially similar features evidence of Northwoods execution or lineage. Provenance requires documentary linkage.

### Redaction is not guilt

COINTELPRO contains significant redactions and the Church Committee corpus includes omissions/context gaps. Redaction location and likely information type should be scored, but a redaction cannot itself be treated as proof of incriminating content.

### Planning is not execution

Northwoods is strong evidence of formal consideration and planning. It is not evidence that the listed incidents were executed. COINTELPRO likewise contains proposals and explicit `no pending action` / `no tangible results` entries alongside records of attempted exploitation.

## First promoted reusable patterns

- `BI-PAT-001` — Purpose drift / objective-first justification
- `BI-PAT-002` — Collection-to-consequence chain
- `BI-PAT-003` — Fragmented oversight / incomplete-chain review

## Candidate controls derived across sources

1. **Independent predicate review:** consequential action cannot rely solely on evidence generated, interpreted, and approved by the same operational chain.
2. **Purpose-binding:** information carries explicit permitted-use metadata; secondary adverse use requires separate authorization.
3. **Proposal/approval/execution/outcome states:** systems must never collapse these into a single `action` state.
4. **Full-chain auditability:** authorized reviewers must be able to reconstruct collection/event source, transformations, attribution, dissemination, approvals, actions, and outcomes.
5. **No-action as a valid outcome:** management systems must not implicitly require intervention or `tangible results` where evidence does not justify action.

## Candidate detections derived across sources

- The same team/system acts as evidence generator, analyst, approver, and executor.
- Desired action appears in planning records before neutral supporting evidence is established.
- Information collected for one purpose is exported into a materially different adverse-action workflow.
- Multiple allegedly independent incidents share uncommon infrastructure, authorship, or approval pathways.
- Proposals lack a recorded disposition, leaving later reviewers unable to distinguish rejected ideas from executed actions.

## Confidence

- Purpose drift / objective-first decisioning: **High**
- Collection-to-consequence chain: **High**
- Fragmented oversight / incomplete-chain review: **High**
- Any claim that these three historical programs share a single hidden command structure: **Unsupported**
