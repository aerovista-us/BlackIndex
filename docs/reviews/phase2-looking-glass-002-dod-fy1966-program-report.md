## Secretary of Defense program-level finding

In the FY1966 operational-forces section, the report describes PACCS as providing airborne launch-control capability for the strategic retaliatory forces if ground-based launch facilities did not survive an initial enemy attack.

The same passage states that additional EC-135 aircraft were added to the PACCS force during the year and describes that expansion as improving command-and-control survivability.

## Secretary of the Air Force program-level finding

The Air Force section describes continued development of a post-attack command-control system intended to preserve control of strategic weapons by the highest levels of authority during general war.

It describes the SAC airborne command post as an EC-135C that could control the retaliatory force if SAC headquarters and alternate ground command posts were destroyed. It also records a general officer aboard as emergency action officer and states that an airborne launch-control center for Minuteman missiles was operational.

The same section notes that SAC computers were being used to help revise the Single Integrated Operations Plan (SIOP). That establishes program proximity, not the contents or authority rules of the SIOP itself.

## Evidentiary value

Compared with the 1991 and 1997 Air Force histories, this source is contemporaneous with the FY1966 PACCS program and therefore materially improves the chronology/capability layer.

It supports a period DoD institutional description of:

- PACCS airborne launch-control capability;
- EC-135 command-and-control survivability;
- airborne command-post continuity if ground command posts were destroyed;
- an operational airborne Minuteman launch-control center;
- a relationship between SAC command-control systems and SIOP planning work.

## Limits

This report does **not** establish the complete nuclear command-and-control authority chain.

It does not, by itself, provide:

- the exact Emergency War Order language;
- the full SIOP execution or authentication procedure;
- who could originate a nuclear-use order under each contingency;
- the conditions for delegation, succession, or predelegation;
- the complete crew checklist or message-authentication process;
- evidence that airborne launch-control capability was used in an operational nuclear event.

Capability, authority, authentication, procedure, and actual use remain separate propositions.

The record is therefore classified `deuterocanon`: a contemporaneous official program report whose capability statements are preserved, while authority/procedure claims remain review-required.
