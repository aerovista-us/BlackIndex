## What the first SAC history supports

The 1991 SAC history states that SAC began testing an airborne command post at Offutt AFB on 1 July 1960. It describes the airborne platform as an alternate command post intended to assume control over the SAC combat force if the underground Offutt facility and other command posts were destroyed.

It states that continuous airborne command-post operations began on 3 February 1961 and that the nickname `Looking Glass` reflected the airborne operation mirroring the underground SAC command post.

The same history states that PACCS was augmented with three auxiliary airborne command posts in April 1962 and later describes EC-135 aircraft assuming Looking Glass and auxiliary airborne-command-post missions in 1965.

It also describes the Airborne Launch Control System as a capability developed to launch land-based Minuteman missiles from the airborne command post, with a 1967 test launch and subsequent initial operational capability.

## What the broader Air Force history supports

The 1997 institutional history independently publishes a similar chronology: Looking Glass as an airborne alternate to the Offutt underground command facility, auxiliary airborne command posts, PACCS expansion, and a 1967 airborne launch-control test from a modified KC-135.

## Source-class limits

Both records are **institutional historical syntheses**. They are not being treated as operational directives or legal/command authorities.

The current corpus does not yet establish, from these two histories alone:

- the exact Emergency War Order language governing Looking Glass crews;
- the authentication chain for nuclear-use messages;
- which authorities could issue, relay, or execute particular orders under each historical configuration;
- the complete relationship among LOOKING GLASS, NEACP/NIGHTWATCH, TACAMO, SIOP/EWO, and later USSTRATCOM command arrangements;
- whether the two histories rely on materially independent underlying archives for each repeated proposition.

Accordingly, both records are classified `deuterocanon` as official institutional research layers. Their authenticity/provenance is mapped, while authority claims and source independence require upstream-record review.

## Evidence-counting rule

A second official history repeating the first history's chronology is not automatically a second independent evidentiary source.

Capability, authority, procedure, and actual use are separate propositions. For example, documentation that an airborne platform possessed launch-control capability does not by itself establish who was authorized to order a launch, under what conditions, or that the capability was used operationally.
