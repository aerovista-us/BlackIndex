# TRAIN-002 — Useful Information, Wrong Purpose

## Source patterns
- `BI-PAT-002` Collection-to-consequence chain
- `BI-PAT-001` Purpose drift / objective-first justification

## Scenario
An internal security analyst discovers embarrassing but non-security-related information while investigating a legitimate alert. A manager suggests quietly providing it to an outside partner who could terminate the subject's access, lease, employment, or business relationship.

## Trainee objectives
1. Identify the purpose shift.
2. Preserve the original evidence without exploiting it.
3. Determine whether a separate legal/policy authority exists for secondary adverse use.
4. Require independent approval before any third-party contact.
5. Document the proposed action and disposition.

## Injects
- The information is verified and true.
- The outside partner is willing to act immediately.
- The original security allegation remains unresolved.

## Pass criteria
- Trainee understands that truth of information does not authorize every use.
- Trainee blocks unauthorized third-party influence.
- Trainee records the attempted escalation and preserves provenance.
- Trainee permits legitimate referral only if a separate documented authority exists.

## Historical teaching basis
The COINTELPRO Alexandria packet documents consideration of using investigative information for media exposure, landlord pressure, legal pressure, and other disruptive outcomes. This scenario extracts the purpose-limitation lesson without equating modern internal-security programs with COINTELPRO.
