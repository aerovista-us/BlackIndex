# TRAIN-001 — The Incident That Fits Too Well

## Source patterns
- `BI-PAT-001` Purpose drift / objective-first justification
- `BI-PAT-003` Fragmented oversight / incomplete-chain review

## Scenario
Leadership already favors a major enforcement action. Over two weeks three apparently unrelated alerts appear and collectively support that desired outcome. Each alert looks plausible in isolation, but the underlying provenance is incomplete.

## Trainee objectives
1. Separate observed facts from desired outcome.
2. Determine whether the alerts are genuinely independent.
3. Identify shared infrastructure, authorship, approval, or collection sources.
4. Construct at least two alternative hypotheses.
5. Define the independent evidence required before escalation.
6. Preserve a valid `no action yet` option.

## Injects
- Alert A was generated by a system configured by the same team requesting enforcement.
- Alert B uses a data source derived from Alert A but is presented as independent.
- Alert C is genuinely independent but ambiguous.

## Pass criteria
- Trainee does not count derivative evidence as independent corroboration.
- Trainee requires independent predicate review.
- Trainee documents proposal/approval/execution states separately.
- Trainee does not infer misconduct merely because the evidence chain is weak.

## Historical teaching basis
Operation Northwoods demonstrates formal planning in which incidents, attribution, and narrative effects could be designed around a desired policy endpoint. This scenario teaches the governance lesson without asserting equivalence to modern events.
